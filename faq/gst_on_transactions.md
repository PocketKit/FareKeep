# How GST Works on Transactions

If your business is registered for the Goods and Services Tax (GST), you need to track the GST component of your income and expenses. FareKeep makes this straightforward.

## What is GST?

GST is a 10% tax on most goods and services sold in Australia. If you're GST-registered, you charge GST on your income and can claim back the GST you pay on business expenses. The difference is what you owe (or are owed) when you lodge your Business Activity Statement (BAS). See [What is a BAS?](business_activity_statement) for more information.

## Using GST in FareKeep

When adding a transaction (income or expense), you'll see a **GST Included** toggle.

### When GST is toggled ON:
- FareKeep assumes the amount you entered **already includes GST**.
- It automatically calculates the GST component as **1/11th** of the total amount.
- For example: If you enter **$110**, the GST is calculated as **$10**, and the GST-exclusive amount is **$100**.

### When GST is toggled OFF:
- The transaction is treated as **GST-free** (e.g., bank fees, some insurance, or if you're not GST-registered).

### Manual Override
If the GST amount on your receipt differs from the automatic calculation (which can happen due to rounding), you can manually edit the GST field to match the exact amount on your receipt.

## Do I Need to Use This?

- **GST-registered businesses:** Yes — toggle GST on for every transaction that includes GST. This ensures your [GST Report](reports_overview) is accurate when it's time to lodge your BAS.
- **Not GST-registered:** You can leave this turned off. Your transactions will still be recorded correctly for income tax purposes.

You can check or update your GST registration status in **Settings → Profile Details**.

*For official guidance on GST, visit the [ATO website](https://www.ato.gov.au).*
