# Linking Expenses to a Vehicle

When you add an expense that is related to a vehicle — such as fuel, tyres, servicing, insurance, or registration — you can link it directly to a specific vehicle in FareKeep. This is important because the ATO treats motor vehicle expenses differently from other business expenses.

## Why Link Expenses to a Vehicle?

Motor vehicle expenses can be claimed under two different ATO methods: **Cents per Kilometre** or the **Logbook Method**. When you generate your Tax Summary Report, FareKeep needs to know which expenses are vehicle-related so it can:

- Group them by vehicle
- Calculate the correct deduction under each method
- Let you compare and choose the method that gives you the best deduction

If you don't link an expense to a vehicle, it will be treated as a general business expense (reported under Item D5 on your tax return) rather than a motor vehicle expense (Item D1).

## How to Link an Expense to a Vehicle

1. When adding or editing an expense, look for the **Related to vehicle?** dropdown in the Basic Details section of the form.
2. By default, it is set to **None** — meaning the expense is not related to any vehicle.
3. Tap the dropdown and select the logbook for the vehicle this expense relates to.
4. Once selected, FareKeep will display helpful information about the logbook's current business-use percentage below the Categorization & Tax section.

## What If I Select "None"?

If you leave the dropdown set to "None", the expense is treated as a normal business expense. This is correct for expenses that have nothing to do with a vehicle — such as office supplies, phone bills, or software subscriptions.

## Does This Affect My Claimable Percentage?

- If you use the **Automatic** claimable method, the logbook's business-use percentage will be applied automatically when you generate your reports.
- If you use the **Manual** claimable method or set the expense to **100% Claimable**, the percentage you enter will be used as-is. However, the expense will still be grouped under that vehicle's motor vehicle deductions in your Tax Summary Report.

## Example

You fill up your car with $80 of fuel. When adding this expense:
1. Set the amount to **$80**.
2. Under **Related to vehicle?**, select the logbook for your car.
3. If your logbook shows 70% business use and you're using Automatic mode, FareKeep will claim $56 as a deduction when generating your tax report.

*For official guidance on motor vehicle expense claims, visit the [ATO website](https://www.ato.gov.au).*
