# How to Record a Trip

Recording every trip you take — both business and personal — is essential for maintaining an accurate logbook. The ATO uses your trip records to determine what percentage of your vehicle expenses you can claim as a tax deduction.

## Adding a New Trip

1. Go to the **Logbooks** tab and tap on the logbook you want to add a trip to.
2. Tap the **+** (plus) button.
3. Fill in the trip details:
   - **Date** — the date of the trip.
   - **Start Odometer** — your car's dashboard reading before you start driving. FareKeep will automatically suggest the end odometer from your last trip.
   - **End Odometer** — your car's dashboard reading when you arrive.
   - **From / To** — where you drove from and to (e.g., "Home" → "Airport").
   - **Business or Personal** — toggle whether this trip was for business or personal use.
   - **Trip Purpose** — select a reason for the trip (e.g., "Client pickup", "Airport run"). You can create new purposes from **Settings → Master Data**.
   - **Description** (optional) — any additional notes.

4. Tap **Save** to record the trip, or **Save as Draft** if you don't have all the details yet.

## How Distance is Calculated

FareKeep automatically calculates the trip distance by subtracting the start odometer from the end odometer. For example:

- Start Odometer: **45,230 km**
- End Odometer: **45,258 km**
- Trip Distance: **28 km**

## Tips

- **Record trips as you go.** It's much easier to jot down your odometer reading before and after each trip than to try to remember it later.
- **Don't skip personal trips.** The ATO requires you to log both business and personal trips during a logbook period so it can calculate an accurate business-use percentage.
- **Use drafts for incomplete trips.** If you're about to start driving and only know the start odometer, save it as a draft and come back to fill in the end reading later.

*For official guidance on the logbook method, visit the [ATO website](https://www.ato.gov.au).*
